# Module 3 — Putting Time in Checkmate

**Retiree Business Blueprint | Mastery Track | Week 3**

---

## What This Module Is About

In Module 2, you built a clear picture of your life and your business — where they are now and where you want them to go — and you wrote five specific objectives to bridge the gap.

Now those objectives need time.

Module 3 is about your schedule. Not time management in the sense of efficiency tricks and productivity systems, but something more foundational: an honest look at where your time is actually going, and the deliberate decisions required to redirect it toward what you have identified as most important.

By the end of this week, you will have a revised working schedule — built around your default settings, your big picture goals, and your business commitments — that you can actually use and actually maintain.

This is the week where your plan gets a home.

---

## What You Will Be Able to Do by the End of This Module

- Map your current weekly schedule honestly, including the habits and patterns that fill time without earning it.
- Identify the six schedule anchors — three from your default settings and three from your big picture goals — that your revised schedule must protect.
- Name and make decisions about the habits, obligations, and patterns that consume time without producing real value in your life.
- Make explicit, written commitments to both your business time and your personal time.
- Build a revised weekly template that gives your week a structure designed around what matters most.

## A Note Before You Begin

Most people who have been through any kind of business or productivity training have been asked to write out their current schedule and build a revised one. Many have done it before — possibly more than once.

This week is different from those exercises for a specific reason: you are not trying to optimize your schedule in the abstract. You are connecting your schedule to the defaults and big picture you have already built over the past two weeks. The schedule you produce on Friday will be designed specifically around who you are, what you value, and where you are trying to go — not around a generic template of what a productive week is supposed to look like.

That specificity is what makes it useful and what makes it likely to survive contact with your real life.
