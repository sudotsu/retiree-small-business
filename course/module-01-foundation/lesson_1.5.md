# Lesson 1.5 — Fit Check and Decision Gate

### Estimated Time: Approximately 70–75 minutes

*(Reading: 25 minutes | Exercises: 30 minutes | Journal: 15 minutes)*

---

## Lesson Overview

Over the past four lessons, you have examined what drives your decisions (1.1), mapped your default settings across four categories (1.2), evaluated the positive and negative dimensions of each setting (1.3), and rewritten the friction-causing ones with guardrails (1.4).

This lesson is the integration point. You will use everything from the week to make one clear, honest decision: given who you are right now — your energy, your resources, your support, your mindset — are you ready to move into Module 2?

Not "ready" as in perfect. Not "ready" as in fear-free. Ready as in: the conditions you actually have today support moving forward in a sustainable way.

The goal of this lesson is not to build momentum through enthusiasm. It is to make the decision that gives your business the best chance of surviving contact with real life.

By the end of Lesson 1.5, you will have:

- A clear, honest assessment of your current readiness across seven areas.
- A written Fit Statement that captures where you are and what your guardrails need to address.
- A specific first action matched to your readiness level.

---

## Part 1 — Why Fit Matters

A business that fits you works with your energy, your health, your finances, your family rhythm, and your temperament. It challenges you, but it does not chew you up.

When a business does not fit — even a well-designed one — the same work feels heavy. Things that should be small problems become large ones because the person doing the work is running against their own grain.

Fit means congruence: your values, your resources, and your time all pointing in the same direction. Not perfectly — that is not realistic. But in enough alignment that the friction you encounter is from the work itself, not from the constant cost of overriding your own defaults.

Today you measure that alignment.

## Exercise 1 — Traffic-Light Readiness Check

For each area below, mark your honest current state: Green (ready), Yellow (some concerns), or Red (not yet). Write brief notes for each.

| Area | What to assess | Color | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|-------|
| **Time and Energy** | Do you have five consistent hours per week you can protect without significant resentment or obligation conflict? | | |
| **Money and Resources** | Can you set aside a modest amount ($50–$500) to test ideas without creating financial stress? | | |
| **Support Network** | Do you have at least one person who will encourage you and at least one who will hold you accountable — and ideally those are different people? | | |
| **Mindset** | Are you genuinely curious about what you do not yet know, and willing to be wrong and learn from it? | | |
| **Technology Comfort** | Can you use basic email and an online course platform, or are you willing to use the Tech Concierge labs to get there? | | |
| **Risk Tolerance** | When plans shift or something does not work, do you adapt and continue, or do you tend to stall? | | |
| **Motivation** | What is stronger right now — the fear of failure, or the desire for what you are building? | | |

Look at the pattern — not as a grade, but as a signal about where your energy is going before you have started.

## Part 2 — Understanding Your Result

### Green — Ready to Move Forward

Your current conditions support moving into Module 2 without significant modification to your approach.

Keep your guardrails from Lesson 1.4 visible. Begin Module 2 with the understanding that your five business objectives (which you will write in Lessons 2.4 and 2.5) will need to fit the energy level and schedule you have now — not the one you are hoping to build toward.

A useful first action: set a small, specific goal for the next thirty days — something you could earn actual money from within that window. Not a finished business. A test. Module 2 will shape what that test looks like.

---

### Yellow — Ready With Guardrails

You are close. A few areas need more support before the pace is sustainable.

Maybe your schedule is inconsistent. Maybe your technology comfort is genuinely low. Maybe your support network has gaps.

Write three sentences that complete this statement: *"I can move forward safely when ____."*

*Examples:*
- "I can move forward safely when I have completed one Tech Concierge lab and feel confident logging in and navigating the course."
- "I can move forward safely when I have protected a consistent hour each weekday morning and held it for two weeks."
- "I can move forward safely when I have one person I have explicitly asked to check in with me weekly."

Review these each week. A Yellow does not mean wait — it means move with awareness of the specific conditions that matter.

---

### Red — Not the Right Time Yet

This is not a stop sign. It is a re-route.

If health, a caregiving situation, financial stress, or a significant life transition is taking primary energy right now, acknowledging that is not failure — it is honesty. Building a business on a depleted foundation is harder than building it when you have more margin.

If you are in a Red situation, the Essentials track (seven days) is a lower-demand way to stay engaged with the material without the pace of the full Mastery track. You can also use this time to complete one specific action per week toward the conditions that would move you to Yellow.

Complete a brief Pause Plan:

- "What needs to change before I resume the full Mastery pace?"
- "What can I do each week during the pause to make forward movement?"
- "When will I reassess this decision?" (Set a calendar reminder for sixty days from now.)

A deliberate pause is not inaction. It is the right decision, made honestly, which is the thing this entire module has been building toward.

## Part 3 — Real Fit in Practice

**Mary — The Encourager Who Refined Her Fit**

Mary rated herself Yellow on time and money when she first reached this lesson. She wanted to coach newly retired professionals, but she was also caring for an aging parent part-time.

Her guardrail: three clients maximum until her expenses were stable and her schedule was predictable.

Six months later she had steady referrals, breathing room in her schedule, and a clearer picture of how much she could expand.

**Evelyn — The Planner Who Paused**

Evelyn rated herself Red on energy while her husband was recovering from surgery. She did not try to power through the Mastery track.

She took the Essentials track instead and used her limited available time to write the first draft of a downsizing guide she had been planning for years.

When her husband's recovery was complete, she was not starting over. She was continuing.

Their lesson is the same: fit is not about ego or ambition. It is about alignment between what you are building and what you actually have to build it with right now.

## Exercise 2 — Your Personal Fit Statement

Complete these four sentences in your journal or workbook. Write full sentences for each — not bullet points, not fragments.

**1.** "My current default patterns support my business because ____."

**2.** "My biggest risk area right now is ____, and my guardrail for it is ____."

**3.** "The type of business that fits my life today is ____ because ____."

**4.** "To stay aware of how my fit is changing, I will review this statement every ____ weeks."

Sign and date your Fit Statement.

Optional: photograph it and post it in the community thread for accountability.

## Journal Prompts

Take fifteen minutes to write full responses to these questions. Full sentences, not bullet points.

**2.** Which guardrail from Lesson 1.4 will matter most in your first weeks of Module 2? Why that one in particular?

**3.** Who is one person you can tell about this decision — not to get advice, but to stay accountable? What specifically will you ask of them?

**4.** How will you know when your fit has improved — when Yellow has become Green, or when your Red conditions have resolved enough to resume? What will you see or feel that tells you?
