# Lesson 1.2 — Mapping Your Default Settings

### Estimated Time: Approximately 75–80 minutes

*(Reading: 30 minutes | Exercises: 30 minutes | Journal: 15 minutes)*

---

## Lesson Overview

In Lesson 1.1 you identified the specific influences that shape how you make decisions and named the three that matter most. Now it is time to see how those influences live inside four specific areas of your personality — your Attitudes, Beliefs, Motivations, and the People whose energy affects your choices.

This lesson is about noticing patterns, not judging them. You are building a snapshot of how you are currently wired so you can decide in Lessons 1.3 and 1.4 which settings to keep, which to adjust, and which to quiet down for the sake of what you are building.

By the end of Lesson 1.2, you will have:

- A written map of your current default settings across four categories.
- A clear picture of which settings energize you and which drain you.
- A starting point for the rewrite you will complete in Lesson 1.4.

---

## Part 1 — The Four Default Categories

These four categories cover the dimensions of your personality that most directly affect how you will build and sustain a business.

| Category | What it represents | Why it matters in business |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Attitudes** | Your default emotional stance toward challenge, change, and uncertainty | Determines whether you experience obstacles as information or as threats |
| **Beliefs** | The truths you have accepted about money, worth, learning, and age | They can fuel action or freeze it; left unexamined, they become invisible ceilings |
| **Motivations** | What energizes you to start, persist, or finish | Helps you design a business model that keeps you engaged over time, not just at launch |
| **People** | The voices whose opinions matter most to you | Their energy either compounds or cancels your own, often without either of you realizing it |

## Exercise 1 — Your Default Settings Map

For each category, list five entries — no filtering, no editing. Write what is actually true right now.

After listing each item, mark it with one of three labels:

- **Helps me** — this setting consistently supports forward movement
- **Sometimes hinders me** — useful under certain conditions, problematic under others
- **Usually hurts me** — this setting reliably creates friction or gets in the way

## Part 2 — Reading Your Map

Once your lists are complete, look across all four categories for patterns.

Circle any item marked "Helps me" that you rely on consistently — these are your active strengths.

Underline any item marked "Sometimes hinders me" that tends to show up when you are tired or under pressure — these are the settings most likely to surprise you.

Put a small mark next to any item marked "Usually hurts me" that appears in more than one category — these are the patterns with the most reach. If the same type of friction shows up in both your beliefs and your people, it is influencing more of your life than you realize.

*Example:*

> Attitude — "I hesitate to try new technology."
>
> Belief — "I will not be taken seriously online."
>
> People — "Friends who dismiss social media."
>
> Combined effect: Technology will be a consistent friction point. Addressing it early — through a Tech Concierge lab, for instance — prevents it from becoming a recurring obstacle.

## Exercise 2 — Energizers and Drainers

Create a simple two-column list in your journal:

| What consistently energizes me (top 3 from "Helps me") | What consistently drains me (top 3 from "Usually hurts me") |
|--------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| | |

Then write a short paragraph — at least three sentences — on each of these questions:

- How could you design your business so the energizers show up often and the drains show up rarely?
- What would change if you simply asked the people who drain you to cheer you on rather than advise you?
- Which single "Sometimes hinders me" item is the most realistic to improve over the next thirty days?

## Mini-Story — John and the Support Conversation

John's strongest motivation was order. He was a planner by nature — detailed, consistent, methodical. He loved structured systems and felt uneasy when things were uncertain.

His wife, Elaine, was the opposite: spontaneous, flexible, and easily frustrated by too many plans. When John was building his consulting practice, Elaine's input on his ideas often came out as skepticism, which he experienced as friction. They argued. Progress slowed.

Then John asked her a different question: not "What do you think of my plan?" but "What would it take for you to feel good about what I am doing?"

She said: "I just want to see you doing something you believe in. I do not need the details."

That one conversation shifted everything. John stopped explaining plans and started sharing results. Elaine stopped questioning processes and started celebrating milestones. His support network went from a source of friction to a source of fuel — without either of them changing who they were.

Sometimes the map is not about changing your settings. It is about changing the conversation around them.

## Journal Prompts

Take fifteen minutes to write full responses to these questions. Full sentences, not bullet points.

**1.** Which of the four categories feels most solid and settled right now — the one that is genuinely working in your favor? What makes it stable?

**2.** Which category feels most out of alignment with the life you want to build? What specifically is misaligned?

**3.** Which belief marked "Usually hurts me" do you think you inherited rather than earned through your own experience? Where did it come from, and is there any honest reason to keep it?

**4.** Who in your current circle seems most genuinely invested in your success — not just supportive of you as a person, but specifically curious about what you are building? How could you involve them this week?

**5.** If you could improve just one category this month, which one would have the largest ripple effect on the others? Why?
