# Day 2 — Painting the Big Picture

### Time: 65–75 minutes
*Reading and reflection: 20 min / Exercise: 35 min / Journal: 10 min*

---

## Overview

Yesterday you looked inward at what is wiring you. Today you look outward at the full picture of your life and your business — what it currently looks like and what you want it to look like.

This is called "painting the big picture" for a reason. You are not writing a business plan today. You are not building a spreadsheet. You are getting a clear, honest view of everything that is in the frame right now — the people, the activities, the roles, the qualities — and deciding how you want that picture to change.

---

## Why This Matters

One of the most common mistakes new business owners make is allowing the business to become the entire picture. It starts small: the business needs attention, so you give it more. Over time it crowds out family, health, relationships, recreation. And then one day you look up and realize that what you built swallowed the life you were trying to improve.

The antidote is doing this exercise before that drift starts. If you paint the picture now — clearly, honestly — you will have a reference point you can return to whenever the business starts taking more than its share.

The exercise uses six categories to describe the picture. Some of them are straightforward. A couple of them are unusual but worth taking seriously.

**People** are everyone who matters in your business life and personal life — customers, family, colleagues, friends. Include people who have a negative impact, not just positive ones. A realistic picture includes both.

**Activities** are what you actually do with your time — at home, with family, at work, with friends, in civic or volunteer roles, in hobbies. Not what you wish you did. What you actually do.

**Roles** are how people see you and how you see yourself. Some roles are defined by family: parent, sibling, grandparent. Others come from what you do. Your business will add a new role to the list. Where does it fit?

**Flavors** are a more unusual prompt, but a useful one. If your life as it stands today left a taste in someone's mouth — including your own — what would that taste be? A meal you are proud of, or something that has gone stale? What taste do you want to leave?

**Colors** ask a similar question in a different register. What colors do you see when you look at your current picture? Where do they come from? What would you add?

**Sounds** ask: when you think about your business and your life right now, do you hear music or noise? Something ordered or chaotic? What would you like to hear?

The flavors, colors, and sounds prompts are not decoration. They are a way of accessing how you feel about your current situation in ways that direct factual description sometimes misses. Do not skip them.

## Earl's Story

Earl retired from thirty years in facilities management and immediately started a handyman service out of his garage. He had the skills, the tools, and more calls than he could handle within the first month. By the third month, he was working fifty-hour weeks, his wife was eating dinner alone most evenings, and he had not been to a single one of his grandchildren's Saturday games.

He had built exactly what he said he did not want to build.

When a friend suggested he sit down and actually describe what his life looked like versus what he wanted it to look like, Earl resisted. He already knew what the problem was, he said. He was just too busy.

His friend pressed him to do it anyway.

When Earl put it on paper — the actual current picture against the one he wanted — the problem was not "too busy." It was that he had never defined what he was building toward. He had a business but no picture. He was filling every available hour because there was no image of what enough looked like.

He cut his client list in half, raised his rates, and added Saturdays back to the family calendar. The business still works. So does the rest of his life.
