# Instructor Guide — Essentials Track

**Retiree Business Blueprint | Essentials Track**

*For Chaz. Not for students.*

---

## Course Overview

The Essentials Track is an eight-day intensive: one exercise per day, approximately one hour per session, with Day 5 running 90–105 minutes due to the financial exercises. Total course time is eight to ten hours.

Each day is self-contained but builds on previous days. Day 8 pulls from all seven prior days — students who skip ahead will find the final exercise significantly less useful.

The course is designed for two primary use cases: a standalone one-week assessment for someone who wants a fast picture of their business, and a free introductory offering that bridges to the Mastery Track.

---

## Day-by-Day Facilitation Notes

### Day 1 — Your Business and Your Default Settings

**Core concept:** Internal wiring — attitudes, beliefs, motivations, key people — shapes everything that follows. Most people have never examined these systematically.

**Facilitation note:** The most common resistance here is from students who feel the exercise is too introspective and just want to "get to the business stuff." Hold the line. Students who skip this exercise often discover six weeks later that the blocker they are fighting was visible on Day 1.

**What to watch for:** Students who rate all four categories positively without much reflection. Push them — specifically on beliefs ("What do you believe about whether someone like you can succeed at this?") and on people ("Is there anyone in your life who has expressed doubt or resistance?").

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 1 (Knowing Yourself) spends a full five lessons on this territory. Day 1 is the entry point; Module 1 is the full depth.

---

### Day 2 — Painting the Big Picture

**Core concept:** External picture — people, activities, roles, flavors, colors, sounds — as it currently is versus as the student wants it to be.

**Facilitation note:** The flavors, colors, and sounds prompts produce the most interesting conversation. Some students dismiss them as abstract; others find them the most revealing part of the exercise. Do not over-explain them — let students respond however they respond and then use what they produce.

**What to watch for:** Students who fill out the "as I want it to be" column with only business-related entries. This is a signal that the business has already started crowding out the rest of the picture. Name it directly.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 2 (Your Big Picture) covers this in full depth over five lessons with additional frameworks for understanding where a business fits in a life.

---

### Day 3 — Putting Time in Checkmate

**Core concept:** A weekly schedule template — not a day-by-day appointment book — that protects business time, family time, and personal time.

**Facilitation note:** Expect resistance from students who feel their schedule "cannot be scheduled" because of irregular commitments. The schedule is a template for a typical week, not a rigid binding plan. The goal is to identify the recurring structure, not to account for every exception.

**What to watch for:** Business blocks that are scattered, small, and not protected. A student who has "maybe a few hours here and there" for the business is likely to find that those hours get filled with other things. A schedule only works if the business time is treated as an appointment.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 3 (Putting Time in Checkmate) spends five lessons on time management frameworks, schedule design, and the psychology of why schedules fail.

---

### Day 4 — Marketing and Promotion

**Core concept:** The five Ps framework — Product, Pricing, Propagation, Positioning, Promotion — as the foundation of the marketing message.

**Facilitation note:** The draft advertisement exercise at the end is optional in the workbook but worth encouraging strongly. A student who cannot write a coherent advertisement for their business has not yet clarified the message well enough. The exercise reveals gaps in the message faster than any discussion.

**What to watch for:** Positioning statements that describe the business rather than distinguish it. "I am a reliable and professional service provider" is a description. "I am the only bookkeeper in this area who specializes in the financial complexity of family trusts" is a position.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 4 (Five Ps Marketing) is five lessons on marketing strategy, pricing psychology, promotional channel selection, and message development.

---

### Day 5 — Cash and Profits

**Core concept:** Two-part exercise — actual cash flow assessment from real statements, then a forward-looking budget with revenues, fixed expenses, variable expenses, and net.

**Facilitation note:** This is the most commonly skipped exercise and the most important one. Students who do not have their bank statements available will be tempted to estimate. Estimates are acceptable for the budget section but should not replace actual data in the cash flow section. Encourage students to get their actual statements before starting Day 5.

**What to watch for:** Students who complete the cash flow table and discover a net negative they were not expecting. This is not uncommon and is not cause for alarm — it is information. Redirect the energy toward the budget section and the adjustments question. A business that is currently losing money can become profitable; a business owner who does not know they are losing money cannot fix the problem.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 5 (The Art of Making Money) covers pricing, profit mechanics, cash flow management, and the difference between revenue and profit over five full lessons.

---

### Day 6 — You Need Leads to Succeed

**Core concept:** The four things you need to know about every lead; twelve lead generation channels; one committed action this week.

**Facilitation note:** The most important part of this exercise is the specific action commitment at the end — not the channel assessment table, which is preparatory. A student who assesses twelve channels and commits to a vague intention ("I will work on word of mouth") has not completed the exercise. The action must be specific: who will be contacted, by when, and how.

**What to watch for:** Students who feel they already "know" their lead generation channel and rush through the table. The value of assessing all twelve is often discovering a channel they had not seriously considered.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 6 (Finding Customers) covers lead generation strategy, channel selection, message development, and conversion mechanics over five lessons.

---

### Day 7 — Keeping Customers

**Core concept:** Five-stage customer relationship strategy — conversion, service, lifetime value, advocacy, radar maintenance.

**Facilitation note:** The lifetime value calculation tends to produce the most impact in this exercise. Most students have never done the math. Once they have, the way they think about individual customer interactions changes. Encourage them to calculate an actual number and write it down.

**What to watch for:** Students whose answers to questions 4 and 5 (advocacy and radar maintenance) are vague — "I will ask for referrals" or "I will stay in touch." Push for the concrete version: who specifically, how specifically, and how often.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 7 (Keeping Customers) covers the full customer relationship arc — converting leads, customer service frameworks, lifetime value mechanics, building an evangelist network, and retention strategy — over five lessons.

---

### Day 8 — Putting It All Together

**Core concept:** Three written behavioral objectives (personal, profits, promotional) with what/how/by when; complete Wrap Up.

**Facilitation note:** The most common failure mode here is objectives that are missing the "how." "I will increase revenue by 20% by December" is a what and a when without a how. It is not an objective — it is a wish with a deadline. Require the how before considering the objective complete. If a student cannot write the how, they need to do more thinking about the mechanism before setting the target.

**Story placeholder:** This lesson does not have a Chaz personal story placeholder. The Walter story is a complete named-character narrative. If you want to add your own story here — an experience with a written plan that you kept versus one you put in a folder — the ideal placement is between the "Why This Matters" section and the Exercise heading. 150–200 words. Not required; the lesson is complete without it.

**Connection to Mastery:** Module 8 (Putting It All Together) is the Mastery capstone — same structure as Day 8 but with eight full modules of work behind it. The depth of the objectives a student can write after the Mastery Track is substantially greater than after the Essentials Track.

**Mastery bridge:** The closing section of Day 8 names the bridge explicitly without a hard sell. The framing is: "You now know where your gaps are — that makes Mastery more useful than it would have been before this week." Adjust the tone of that closing if you want it softer or more direct.

## Assessment Standards

Each day has a mini-quiz (5 questions, 80% passing). The checklist for each day is the primary completion marker — quizzes supplement but do not replace the exercise outputs.

A student who completes the quiz but skips the exercise has not completed the day. The exercise is the product.

---

## The Objectives Quality Standard

Any student completing Day 8 should be able to show:

- Three objectives, each with a clear what, a concrete how, and a specific by-when date
- A Wrap Up with three named priorities and at least two people to contact
- A stated location for the document and a written 90-day review date

Objectives missing any of the three fields should be revised before the day is marked complete.
