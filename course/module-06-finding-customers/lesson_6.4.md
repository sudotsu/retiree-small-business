# Lesson 6.4 — Matching Media to Your Customer

### Estimated Time: Approximately 75–80 minutes

*(Reading: 30 minutes | Exercises: 25 minutes | Journal: 15 minutes)*

---

## Lesson Overview

You have evaluated twelve lead generation channels. You have a detailed profile of your typical customer. You have a message tailored to that customer.

Now comes the pairing: which of those channels will actually reach the person you described?

A message can be excellent and still produce nothing if it runs in a channel your customers do not inhabit. This is one of the most common and least-examined failures in small business marketing — not a weak message, but a correct message distributed to the wrong place. The customer never saw it.

By the end of Lesson 6.4, you will have:

- A filtered list of the twelve channels rated specifically against your customer profile, not just your general preferences.
- A distinction between channels that produce leads from the first activation and those that build over time.
- A shorter, more deliberate candidate list to carry into Lesson 6.5, where you will build the one-year plan.

---

## Part 1 — The Profile as a Media Filter

Every characteristic in your customer profile has channel implications. Running through them systematically removes guesswork from the selection process.

**Age and media habits.** Where your customer's age range falls largely determines which platforms and media they actively use. Adults 65 and older are significantly more likely to use Facebook than Instagram or TikTok, more likely to read a local newspaper than to consume news exclusively online, and more likely to respond to direct mail than to digital ads. These are not universal rules — your profile may show exceptions — but they are well-documented patterns worth testing against what you know about your specific customers.

**Geography.** If your customer base is concentrated in a specific zip code or neighborhood, local media carry more weight: community papers, local radio, neighborhood Facebook groups, direct mail to a defined radius. If your customers are spread across a broader region, local media lose some of their efficiency and broader channels become relatively more useful.

**Interests and affiliations.** The third question on your customer profile — what other interests do your customers have — points directly at channels. A customer base with strong church or community organization affiliation suggests bulletin boards, newsletters, and word-of-mouth networks inside those communities. A customer base with specific hobby interests may be reachable through clubs, events, or publications tied to those hobbies.

**Influencer relationships.** The second profile question asked who influences your customers. Those influencers are a channel in themselves. If a specific doctor, financial advisor, attorney, or community figure carries weight with your customers, reaching that influencer is often more efficient than trying to reach each customer directly. One relationship with a trusted referral source can produce more qualified leads than six months of advertising.

**Income level.** Income affects which channels your customers are likely to use, not just which ones you can afford. A customer base with modest or fixed income is more likely to use free community resources, more likely to respond to a clearly stated value offer, and less likely to find you through platforms they do not actively use.

## Part 2 — Match from Day One vs. Builds Over Time

Not all channels work on the same timeline. Before you commit to a channel, know which category it falls into — because your cash flow and patience requirements are different for each.

**Channels that can produce leads from Day One:** Direct mail to a targeted list, a well-placed newspaper or magazine ad, a paid radio spot, a targeted Facebook ad campaign, and a personal outreach call to a permission-based contact list. These channels can produce a response the week they launch.

**Channels that build over time:** Word-of-mouth and referral networks, a website and its search visibility, a social media presence, a newsletter list, community visibility through events or press. These channels are not ineffective — many of the strongest long-term lead sources fall here — but they require sustained, consistent effort before producing reliable volume.

The distinction matters for planning. If you need leads in the next thirty days, you weight toward Day One channels. If you are building a sustainable customer pipeline for the next several years, you invest in the channels that compound over time — while using a Day One channel to keep the short-term pipeline moving.

Most businesses that build durable customer volume use both: a referral and community network built steadily over years, supported by a direct activation channel they can use when they need a more immediate result.

## Mini-Story — Douglas and the Publication Nobody Read

Douglas had spent his career as a controller at a mid-size manufacturing company before retiring and opening a bookkeeping service for small local businesses. When it came time to advertise, he defaulted to what felt familiar: a small display ad in a regional trade publication read primarily by other accounting and finance professionals.

The logic made sense to him. He knew that audience. He had read the publication himself for years.

His customer profile told a different story. His actual clients — the ones who called and hired him and referred their friends — were owners of small retail shops, restaurants, and service businesses. Most were 55 to 72 years old. They were not reading trade accounting publications. They were attending the local chamber of commerce meetings, asking their attorneys for recommendations, and looking up "bookkeeper near me" on their phones.

Douglas cancelled the trade publication ad. He joined the chamber, asked his attorney to mention his name when clients brought up bookkeeping questions, and had a simple one-page website built. Within three months he had more new clients than the previous year combined.

The service was the same. The channels were different.
