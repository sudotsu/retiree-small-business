# Module 0 Instructor Guide — Orientation

**Retiree Business Blueprint | Mastery Track | Orientation Week**

**For:** Chaz Korb (Instructor)

---

## Module Overview and Instructor Framing

Module 0 is the front door. Students arrive here before anything else — before they know how to navigate the course, before they have set any expectations, and before they have invested anything beyond the decision to enroll.

The two things that determine whether a student stays through Week 1 are: whether they believe the technical side is manageable, and whether they believe this course is actually designed for someone like them. Both of those decisions happen in Module 0.

This is not a content week. Nothing here is hard. But the warmth and practicality of how students are met here sets the tone for everything that follows.

## Lesson-by-Lesson Notes

---

### Lesson 0.1 — Welcome and Course Orientation

**Core concept:** The course is manageable, you are supported, and the first decisions (track selection, schedule, workspace) are simple and reversible.

**Timing:** ~60–70 minutes. This is the shortest lesson in the course, by design. Orientation should not feel like work.

**The track decision.** Some students will arrive with anxiety about choosing Essentials vs. Mastery. Reassure them — in community facilitation and in any welcome video — that neither choice is permanent. Students can move between tracks. The more important question is not which track, but whether they will protect an hour each day to actually do it.

**Common student patterns:**

- **Tech anxiety before anything has gone wrong.** The fastest response is practical: "You only need to know how to click one button today. Everything else can wait." Pointing directly to the Tech Concierge option (a real person, a scheduled call) resolves this faster than any explanation of the technology itself.
- **Skipping the workspace exercise.** The workspace setup feels small. Students often skip it or do it mentally rather than physically. It matters more than it appears to — having a consistent spot reduces the number of decisions a student has to make each time they begin. If you see students struggling with consistency in Week 2, this is often a Week 0 skip showing up later.

---

### Lesson 0.2 — Mindset and Preparation for Success

**Core concept:** Before skills, before strategy — honestly examining and updating the beliefs that will determine whether the student stays engaged when things get difficult.

**Timing:** ~70–75 minutes. The Belief Inventory is the part that takes longer than expected, particularly for students who have not done reflective writing before.

**The Second-Act Declaration.** This exercise produces something students will return to. In Week 3 and Week 5, when students encounter exercises that feel uncomfortable or demanding, the declaration is the thing that brings them back to why they started. Encourage students to put it somewhere physical — not just in the workbook, but on a wall, a mirror, a phone screen.

**Common student patterns:**

- **Students who write their declaration for an external audience.** "I want to build a business so my family sees that I still have something to contribute." These declarations work for a while and then collapse when the family's response is not what the student hoped. Gently redirect: the declaration is for you, not for anyone watching. What do *you* need this chapter to mean?
- **Students who mark every belief H or N.** If a student cannot identify any unhelpful beliefs in the inventory, they are either genuinely in a very clear headspace (possible) or they are avoiding the exercise (more common). Either way, they will encounter the beliefs in Lessons 1.2 and 1.3 — the Module 1 work will surface them whether or not the student named them here.

## Structural Note on Module 0 Lessons

The detailed Module 0 source spec (`Module_0_Welcome_Course_Setup`) describes four lessons:

- **0.1** — Welcome, support, tech-light promise (20 min)
- **0.2** — Course tour and community (20 min)
- **0.3** — Readiness quiz and personalized next step (30 min)
- **0.4** — Intent statement, reflection, and calendar commitment (20 min)

The current lesson files (lesson_0.1.md and lesson_0.2.md) consolidate much of this into two fuller lessons. Whether to expand to four shorter lessons is a structural decision — four shorter lessons may work better for students with limited digital experience, since each one has a clear and narrow focus. Two fuller lessons are a simpler production and platform setup.

Neither approach is wrong. The current two-lesson version is complete and usable as-is.

## Suggested Office Hours Topics for Orientation Week

- "I am not sure which track to choose."
- "I am having trouble logging in or finding things on the course."
- "I do not have a business idea yet — should I still start?"
- "My family is not supportive. How do I handle that?"

For all of these: the orientation week answer is almost always reassurance and a specific next step. "You can decide the track later. Do Lesson 0.1 first." "Let's get the Tech Concierge on a call with you right now." "Module 1 is specifically for people who do not know what business fits them yet." "Module 0 already started addressing the unsupportive family question — look at your Belief Inventory."

## Video Content Notes

If recording a welcome video for Module 0 (2–3 minutes suggested):

1. Introduce yourself and name the course directly — no lengthy preamble.
2. Acknowledge that starting something new in this chapter of life is both exciting and occasionally strange-feeling. Name it honestly.
3. Say clearly what the course is not: it is not a hustle program. It is not for people who want to build a tech startup. It is for people who have real experience and want to build something modest, meaningful, and sustainable.
4. Tell them the one thing they should do after the video: open Lesson 0.1 and pick a track.

Keep it under three minutes. The warmth matters more than the information in a welcome video.

## Assessment Notes

Module 0 has no graded assessment. The deliverable is the Lesson 0.2 Second-Act Declaration — signed, dated, placed somewhere visible. The community introduction post from Lesson 0.1 is the accountability marker.

Students who do not complete the Second-Act Declaration in Module 0 often struggle to re-engage with the purpose question when it resurfaces in Module 2. It is worth flagging in the community thread if a student reaches Module 1 without it.
