# Lesson 0.2 — Mindset and Preparation for Success

### Estimated Time: Approximately 70–75 minutes

*(Reading: 30 minutes | Exercises: 25 minutes | Journal: 15 minutes)*

---

## Lesson Overview

Before you build a business, you have to believe it is possible.

And before you believe in the business, you need to be honest about what you believe about yourself — particularly the parts of that belief system that were formed decades ago and have not been examined since.

This lesson is about preparing your inner environment: your mindset, your assumptions about your own age and capability, and your personal expectations for what this course is going to require. The goal is not to become optimistic. It is to become clear.

By the end of Lesson 0.2, you will have:

- Identified the specific beliefs about age, risk, and learning that may create friction as you move through this course.
- Reframed at least one limiting belief into a version that serves you better.
- Written a personal declaration about why this chapter of life matters to you — in your own words, for yourself.

*"The older I get, the more I realize success is less about speed, and more about sustainability."*

---

## Part 1 — The Identity Shift of Starting Something New

Starting a business in retirement is not just a practical challenge. It is an identity challenge.

For decades, your identity was shaped largely by roles others assigned you — your job title, your family role, your place in the organizations you belonged to. Retirement changed some of that. Now you are in the process of building something you design yourself, on your own terms.

That process requires three specific mindset shifts. Not dramatic transformations — just practical updates.

| Shift | From | To |
|-------|------|----|
| **Permission** | "I am too old for this." | "I have lived through more than most of the people I will serve." |
| **Confidence** | "What if I make mistakes?" | "Mistakes are evidence that I am doing something real." |
| **Identity** | "I am retired." | "I am in a second chapter — one I get to write." |

Every module in this course reinforces these shifts through action, not through lectures. You will apply, reflect, and build — and the mindset will follow the work.

## Exercise 1 — The Belief Inventory

The following exercise takes about fifteen minutes. The goal is honesty, not polish.

**Step 1:** In your journal, complete these sentences with whatever comes up first — do not edit:

1. "People my age usually do not ____."
2. "I have always been uncomfortable with ____."
3. "If I try this and it fails, then ____."
4. "My family probably thinks I should just ____."
5. "When I imagine success in this, I picture ____."

**Step 2:** Read back what you wrote. Next to each sentence, mark:

- **H** — if it is a **helpful** belief that supports moving forward
- **N** — if it is **neutral** — neither helping nor hindering
- **U** — if it is **unhelpful or untrue** — something that would slow you down if left unexamined

You now have a self-generated inventory of internal friction. These are the beliefs worth watching as the course progresses.

---

## Part 2 — Replacing Friction with Clarity

For every belief you marked U — unhelpful or untrue — try rewriting it in plain language.

Not into a motivational slogan. Into something that is simply more accurate.

| Old belief | More accurate version |
|-----------|----------------------|
| "I am not good with technology." | "I have figured out harder things than this. I can learn one tool at a time." |
| "No one would buy from someone my age." | "My experience makes me more credible, not less, to the people I am trying to reach." |
| "I cannot sell." | "Selling is explaining something clearly to someone who needs it. I have done that my whole life." |

You will revisit these reframes throughout the course. This is not a one-time exercise — it is the beginning of a habit.

The work here is not inspirational. It is practical. The beliefs you carry into Week 1 will shape every decision you make. Updating them now costs you twenty minutes. Discovering their interference in Week 6 costs you something harder.

## Exercise 2 — Your Second-Act Declaration

This is a personal mission statement. Not for customers, not for the course community — for you.

Write one paragraph answering these three questions:

- Why does building a small business matter to you at this point in your life?
- What will it allow you to feel, become, or contribute?
- What kind of future do you want to prove is still possible?

Start with: *"In this chapter of my life, I choose to build ____ so that I can ____."*

Write the full paragraph. Do not bullet-point it. Then put it somewhere you will see it regularly — taped to your workspace, saved as your phone background, written on the first page of your workbook.

This paragraph is your true north for every difficult week ahead.

## Mini-Story — Robert's Reframe

Robert was a retired electrician who had not touched a computer until age 61. When he decided he wanted to start a YouTube channel teaching older homeowners how to use tools safely, his adult children thought it was an interesting idea that would probably not go anywhere.

Robert wrote this down before he started: *"In this chapter of my life, I choose to teach so that I stay useful, sharp, and helpful — not invisible."*

Twelve months later, he was earning money through tool consultations, and one of his biggest supporters was his skeptical son. The declaration had not predicted that outcome. It had kept him going through the months when nothing was working yet.

Mindset does not change your business overnight. It determines whether you are still in the room when the business starts to work.

## Journal Prompts

Take fifteen minutes to write full responses to these questions. Full sentences, not bullet points.

**1.** Which of the beliefs you marked U surprises you most — the one you least expected to find there? What is its history? Where did it come from?

**2.** What does success look like in this course if it has nothing to do with money? What would tell you, in Week 8, that this was worth the time?

**3.** How will you know when your confidence has grown? What specific signs will you notice — in how you think, how you talk about your work, how you respond to doubt?

**4.** What is one ritual — morning, weekly, monthly — that will help you reset when you feel stuck or doubtful? Be specific about when it happens and what it involves.
