# Module 4 — Marketing: Five P's in a Pod

**Retiree Business Blueprint | Mastery Track | Week 4**

---

## What This Module Is About

In the first three modules, you built the internal infrastructure of your business: who you are and what drives your decisions, where your business fits inside the full picture of your life, what you want to accomplish and by when, and how your weekly schedule is structured to give your business real time.

Module 4 turns outward.

This week is about marketing — specifically, the five dimensions of marketing that determine whether customers find your business, understand what you offer, decide to buy, and tell others about you. Not marketing in the abstract. Not a theory of branding. Five concrete, practical questions you need to answer for your specific product and your specific customers, in order.

The framework is called the Five P's: Product, Price, Propagation, Position, and Promotion. By the end of this week, you will have worked through all five for your business — building a complete marketing foundation you can use and refine for as long as you are in business.

---

## What You Will Be Able to Do by the End of This Module

- Evaluate your product honestly from your customer's perspective using the RAW framework — Relevance, Accessibility, and Worth — and identify the gap between how you see it and how they see it.
- Set a working price for your product using a three-step process grounded in customer value, your actual costs, and your competitive context.
- Map the complete path your product takes from your business to your customer — every step, every cost, every friction point — and identify where the path can be simplified.
- Analyze the position your business currently occupies in your customers' minds across five key dimensions, and identify the position you want to earn.
- Build the four-component promotional message for your business and a basic promotion plan — including a first draft advertisement in real language for your real product.

## A Note Before You Begin

Marketing has a reputation for complexity that it does not deserve. Decades of people who made their living by making marketing seem complicated have contributed to that reputation. For a small business — especially one that is built around real expertise, real relationships, and a real reason for existing — marketing is simpler than it appears.

The Five P's framework does not tell you everything about marketing. It tells you the five things you most need to have figured out before you spend time or money promoting your business. If any one of the five is unexamined, your marketing will be weaker than it needs to be, and you will not be able to diagnose why.

Work through the five lessons in order. Do not skip ahead to Promotion. The promotional message you build in Lesson 4.5 will only be as strong as the product, price, propagation path, and position that support it.
