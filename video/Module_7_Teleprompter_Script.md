# Teleprompter Script — Module 7 Intro
# Keeping Customers

_(Camera on. Warm lighting. Chas seated, relaxed, direct eye contact.)_

---

Every business eventually discovers

that finding customers and keeping them

are two completely different skills —

[pause 1s]

and that most of the attention goes to the wrong one.

[pause 2s]

Lead generation is visible.

You can measure it.

Every new inquiry feels like progress.

[pause 1s]

Retention is quieter.

[pause 1s]

A customer who returns for the fourth time

doesn't register as a win the way a new customer does —

even though the economics are almost always better.

[pause 1s]

No acquisition cost.

Higher trust.

Faster decision.

Stronger likelihood of referral.

[pause 1s]

For most small businesses,

the back end is where durable revenue is built.

[pause 1s]

This week is the back end.

[pause 2s]

---

One number this week will ask you to calculate

that changes how you think about everything else.

[pause 1s]

The lifetime value of a customer.

[pause 1s]

Not the value of a single transaction —

the full value of a retained relationship over time.

[pause 1s]

Take your average transaction value.

Multiply it by how often a typical customer buys from you in a year.

Then multiply that by how many years a well-treated customer tends to stay.

[pause 1s]

For a business where the average transaction is two hundred dollars,

the customer buys twice a year,

and retained customers tend to stay five or six years —

[pause 1s]

the lifetime value of a single customer

is somewhere between two thousand and twenty-four hundred dollars.

[pause 1s]

Looked at that way,

a follow-up call or a genuine response to a complaint

carries a very different weight

than it does when you're thinking transaction by transaction.

[pause 2s]

Most business owners significantly undervalue their existing customers

and significantly overvalue new ones —

simply because the cost of finding new customers is visible,

and the value of keeping existing ones is never aggregated.

[pause 2s]

Patricia, in one of this week's stories,

had run a floral design studio for nine years.

[pause 1s]

She had never calculated what a single retained customer was worth.

[pause 1s]

When she finally did the arithmetic,

some of her longest-standing clients

had each contributed between four and six thousand dollars to her business.

[pause 1s]

She had been treating those clients exactly like new orders.

No follow-up. No check-in.

No acknowledgment of what their loyalty had meant.

[pause 1s]

She wrote letters to her top twenty clients.

Several wrote back.

Two brought new business within the month.

One had been thinking about reducing her orders

and changed her mind.

[pause 1s]

She had not changed the flowers.

She had changed how she thought about the people who ordered them.

[pause 2s]

---

Five lessons.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 7.1** — turning leads into customers.

[pause 1s]

A written conversion pitch

built from six specific elements

drawn from your Module 6 customer profile and your Module 4 message.

Bring those materials.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 7.2** — customer service as a system.

[pause 1s]

All five components of how customers experience your business:

first contact, transaction, after-sale, problem handling,

and ongoing relationship.

[pause 1s]

A service strategy is not about being nice.

Nice varies.

Consistent keeps customers.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 7.3** — the lifetime value calculation.

[pause 1s]

Your numbers. Your business.

The result will change how you evaluate

every service decision you make from here forward.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 7.4** — evangelists.

[pause 1s]

How to turn your most enthusiastic customers

into active advocates for your business.

[pause 1s]

Before you open this lesson,

have five names in mind —

customers whose relationship with your business

has gone beyond transactional.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 7.5** — the retention strategy.

[pause 1s]

Five components. A complete written plan.

How to stay visible to your customers between transactions

so they don't drift to someone who simply stayed in front of them.

[pause 2s]

---

Review your Module 6 materials before opening Lesson 7.1.

Have five customer names ready for Lesson 7.4.

[pause 1s]

The front end brings them in.

This week is what makes them stay.

[pause 2s]

_(fade out)_

Consistent keeps customers" is a line worth landing
