# Teleprompter Script — Module 6 Intro
# Finding Customers

_(Camera on. Warm lighting. Chas seated, relaxed, direct eye contact.)_

---

There is a mistake most businesses make at some point,

and it shows up in a specific way.

[pause 1s]

You ask them who their customer is,

and they say:

anyone who needs what we offer.

[pause 1s]

That sounds like ambition.

It is actually the absence of a strategy.

[pause 1s]

Marketing to everyone

is functionally identical to marketing to no one.

[pause 2s]

---

This week you build the alternative.

[pause 2s]

---

One comparison this week's material uses

that earns its place:

[pause 1s]

A criminal profiler does not guess.

[pause 1s]

They study the evidence —

the patterns that emerge across multiple incidents —

and they build a description

that narrows the field from everyone to someone specific.

[pause 1s]

That narrowing is not a limitation.

It is what makes the search tractable.

[pause 2s]

You need to do the same thing with your customers.

[pause 1s]

Not because they are criminals.

[pause 1s]

Because they have patterns —

habits, characteristics, media preferences, geographies —

that, assembled together,

give you a clear picture of who is actually most likely to buy.

[pause 1s]

Without that picture,

every marketing decision is guesswork dressed as strategy.

[pause 2s]

---

Carl, in one of this week's stories,

had been running a financial coaching practice for two years

with modest, inconsistent results.

[pause 1s]

When he built a proper customer profile,

what emerged was considerably more specific than he expected —

and considerably different from who he had been marketing to.

[pause 1s]

He had been describing a broad category

while serving a narrow one.

[pause 1s]

He strengthened three referral relationships,

changed his website language,

wrote one article for a church newsletter.

[pause 1s]

His new client intake doubled within four months.

[pause 1s]

Not because he had changed what he did.

Because he had finally described who it was for.

[pause 2s]

---

Five lessons.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 6.1** — the toolkit.

[pause 1s]

Twelve lead generation channels

evaluated against your specific business and budget.

[pause 1s]

The goal is to reduce twelve possibilities

to a manageable shortlist

before you commit to anything.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 6.2** — the customer profile.

[pause 1s]

Nine questions.

Age range. Geography. Media habits. Influencers. Income level.

[pause 1s]

Answer these from actual observation of real customers —

not assumptions about who you would like your customers to be.

[pause 1s]

If you have existing customers,

think about your five most recent ones

before you open this lesson.

Their actual characteristics are the raw material.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 6.3** — the message to your profiled customer.

[pause 1s]

You will take the core product message from Module 4

and sharpen it specifically for the customer you just described.

[pause 1s]

A message that speaks to everyone in general terms

reaches your profiled customer as background noise.

[pause 1s]

A message that speaks to their specific age, habits, and concerns

reaches them as something addressed directly to them.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 6.4** — the media match.

[pause 1s]

Your twelve channels, filtered through your customer profile.

[pause 1s]

Some channels that seemed attractive in Lesson 6.1

won't pass this filter.

Some you may have dismissed will make more sense

once you know your customer's actual media habits.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 6.5** — the plan.

[pause 1s]

A one-year lead generation calendar —

your top channels, activation months, costs,

and a first thirty-day commitment.

[pause 1s]

Before you open this lesson,

look up actual prices for the channels you intend to use.

[pause 1s]

A strategy built on guessed costs

will require revision the moment you try to act on it.

[pause 2s]

---

Bring your Module 4 materials —

specifically the product message from Lesson 4.5.

You will revise it in Lesson 6.3.

[pause 1s]

Then open 6.1.

Work through the twelve channels honestly.

[pause 1s]

By the end of this week

you will have a profile, a sharpened message,

and a written plan with real costs attached.

[pause 2s]

_(fade out)_

---

### Production Notes

- **Run time:** 3:30–4:00 at relaxed pace (~110–115 words/min spoken)
- **Camera:** locked mid-shot, eye level, soft warm light
- **Delivery:** the criminal profiler analogy is the conceptual anchor — deliver it straight, not as an aside; "That narrowing is not a limitation — it is what makes the search tractable" should feel like a genuine insight, not a slogan; Carl's story lands the concept practically; the close is action-oriented, no warmth tax on it
- **Slide cues (optional):**
  - [SLIDE 1] "Module 6 — Finding Customers"
  - [SLIDE 2] "Marketing to everyone = marketing to no one"
  - [SLIDE 3] "Build the profile. Evidence, not assumptions."
  - [SLIDE 4] "The 9 Questions"
  - [SLIDE 5] "A specific strategy you will actually follow"
