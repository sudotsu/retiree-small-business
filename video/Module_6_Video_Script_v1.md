# Module 6 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Module:** Finding Customers
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~7–7:30 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~920 words

---

There is a mistake most businesses make at some point, and it shows up in a specific way.

You ask them who their customer is, and they say: anyone who needs what we offer.

That sounds like ambition. It is actually the absence of a strategy. Marketing to everyone is functionally identical to marketing to no one — your message does not land because it is not aimed at anything specific, your channel choices are a guess, and you spend time and money reaching people who were never going to buy from you anyway. The result is activity that feels like marketing and produces almost nothing.

This week you build the alternative.

---

Two weeks ago you built your marketing foundation: a clear product, a grounded price, a mapped delivery path, a defined position, and a first promotional message. Last week you built the financial picture of your business. This week those two threads converge, because the most important question in lead generation is not which channel to use — it is who exactly you are trying to reach, and where those specific people actually are when they are ready to hear from you.

---

One comparison this week's material uses that earns its place: a criminal profiler does not guess. They study the evidence — the details that connect one case to the next, the behavioral patterns that emerge across multiple incidents — and they build a description that narrows the field from everyone to someone specific. That narrowing is not a limitation. It is what makes the search tractable.

You need to do the same thing with your customers. Not because they are criminals. Because they have patterns — habits, characteristics, media preferences, influencers, geographies — that, assembled together, give you a clear picture of who is actually most likely to buy what you offer. Without that picture, every marketing decision is guesswork dressed as strategy.

Carl, in one of this week's stories, had been running a financial coaching practice for two years with modest, inconsistent results. When he finally built a proper customer profile, what emerged was considerably more specific than he expected — and considerably different from who he had been marketing to. He had been describing a broad category while serving a narrow one. He strengthened three specific referral relationships, changed his website language, and wrote one article for a church newsletter. His new client intake doubled within four months. Not because he had changed what he did. Because he had finally described who it was for.

---

Five lessons.

Lesson 6.1 is the toolkit — twelve lead generation channels evaluated against your specific business and budget. Not a generic ranking of channels. An honest assessment of which of the twelve are realistic and worthwhile for your type of business, your customer, and what you can actually invest in time and money. The goal is to reduce twelve possibilities to a manageable shortlist before you commit to anything.

Lesson 6.2 is where you build the profile. Nine questions about your customers — their age range, where they live, what media they use, who influences them, what other interests they have, and what their income level is. Answer these from actual observation of real customers, not assumptions about who you would like your customers to be. The profile is only as useful as it is accurate, and accurate means based on evidence. If you have existing customers, think about your five most recent ones before you open this lesson — their actual characteristics are the raw material.

Lesson 6.3 takes the core product message you built in Module 4 and sharpens it specifically for the customer you profiled. A message that speaks to everyone in general terms will reach your profiled customer as background noise. A message that speaks to their specific age, habits, concerns, and language will reach them as something addressed directly to them. This lesson is the bridge between knowing your customer and speaking to them usefully.

Lesson 6.4 is the media match — running all twelve channels from Lesson 6.1 through the filter of the customer you profiled in Lesson 6.2. Some channels that seemed attractive in Lesson 6.1 will not pass this filter because your customers do not inhabit them. Some channels you may have dismissed will make more sense once you know your customer's actual media habits. The match is the mechanism that keeps you from spending resources on channels that can't reach the person you just spent Lesson 6.2 describing.

Lesson 6.5 is the plan — a one-year lead generation calendar with your top channels, activation months, estimated costs, and a specific thirty-day commitment. Before you open this lesson, look up actual prices for the channels you intend to use. The plan is only a plan if it is grounded in what things actually cost. A strategy built on price estimates you guessed at will require revision the moment you try to act on it.

---

Bring your Module 4 materials to this week — specifically the core product message you built in Lesson 4.5. You will revise it in Lesson 6.3 for the customer you are about to profile.

Then open 6.1. Work through the twelve channels honestly. By the end of this week you will have a profile, a sharpened message, and a written plan with real costs attached — not a general intention to market more, but a specific strategy you will actually follow.
