# Teleprompter Script — Module 8 Intro
# Putting It All Together

_(Camera on. Warm lighting. Chas seated, relaxed, direct eye contact.)_

> ⚠️ **CHAS — SEE LESSON 8.5 BEFORE PUBLISHING**
> Lesson 8.5 has a personal story placeholder (150–200 words) that must be filled before that lesson goes live. Nothing else is blocked. See `lesson_8.5.md` and `module_8_instructor_guide.md`.

---

There is a jazz club near a certain college town

where the music is always exceptional —

always fresh,

always sounding like it is being invented in the room.

[pause 1s]

A musician who plays there regularly

was asked once how they did it.

[pause 1s]

The answer was immediate:

practice.

[pause 2s]

Not improvisation as inspiration.

[pause 1s]

Improvisation as thousands of hours of practice

combined in new ways in real time.

[pause 1s]

The improvisation is built on structure.

The structure is what makes the freedom possible.

[pause 2s]

You have spent seven weeks building that structure.

[pause 1s]

This week is where you put it to work.

[pause 2s]

---

Module 8 is not more analysis.

The analysis is done.

[pause 1s]

The question this week asks is a different one:

[pause 1s]

given everything you have built —

what are you actually committing to?

[pause 2s]

Walter, in one of this week's stories,

had spent twenty-six years doing strategy work at the executive level.

[pause 1s]

He was not someone who needed to be sold on planning.

[pause 1s]

His first two business plans were detailed and thoughtful.

They sat in a folder, unrevised,

from the day he wrote them.

[pause 1s]

In his third year,

he printed his objectives on a single page

and taped it to the inside of his office door.

[pause 1s]

He saw it every time he left the room.

[pause 1s]

"The plan didn't get better the third year," he said.

"I just finally put it somewhere I couldn't ignore it."

[pause 2s]

---

Five lessons.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 8.1** — managing for success.

[pause 1s]

The three areas that determine whether a business sustains itself:

people, things, and money.

[pause 1s]

Where you are strong. Where you have gaps.

One specific improvement in each area.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 8.2** — knowing when you need help.

[pause 1s]

Which of your current responsibilities are worth paying someone else to handle?

Which outside relationships would benefit your business most?

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 8.3** — your long-term strengths.

[pause 1s]

Not what you're good at in general.

What is genuinely sustainable about your specific business over years —

and what your realistic pace actually looks like.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 8.4** — your exit strategy, first draft.

[pause 1s]

Where does this business go eventually?

Who carries it forward?

You don't need final answers.

You need to have thought about them

enough that your current decisions are pointed in the right direction.

[pause 1s]

**Lesson 8.5** — your business action plan.

[pause 1s]

A review of all eight modules.

At least three formal objectives:

personal, profits, promotional.

[pause 1s]

Each one with what, how, and by when.

[pause 1s]

Then you print it.

And you put it somewhere you cannot ignore it.

[pause 2s]

---

Bring everything to this week.

Have your workbooks from all eight modules accessible

before you open Lesson 8.5.

[pause 1s]

Open 8.1.

[pause 1s]

You have done the work.

This week you put it somewhere you can act on it.

[pause 2s]

_(fade out)_

### Production Notes

- **Run time:** 3:30–4:00 at relaxed pace (~110–115 words/min spoken)
- **Camera:** locked mid-shot, eye level, soft warm light
- **Delivery:** the jazz analogy opens the final module — deliver "The structure is what makes the freedom possible" as the thesis of the whole course, not just this week; Walter's quote ("I just finally put it somewhere I couldn't ignore it") should land as practical wisdom, not anecdote; the close should have finality without ceremony — this is the last intro before the course closes, let the weight sit without overdressing it
