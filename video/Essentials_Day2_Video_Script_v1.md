# Essentials Day 2 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Day:** Day 2 — Painting the Big Picture
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~590 words

---

Yesterday you looked inward at what is wiring you. Today you look outward at everything in the frame: your life as it actually is right now, and the way you want it to look.

This exercise has a name. Painting the big picture. Not writing a business plan. Not building projections. Getting a clear, honest view of all the people, activities, roles, and qualities in your current situation — and deciding how you want that picture to change.

It sounds like the soft part of the week. It is not. It is the part that determines whether the business serves your life, or eventually replaces it.

---

Earl retired from thirty years in facilities management and immediately launched a handyman service. He had the skills. The demand was immediate. By month three he was working fifty-hour weeks, his wife was eating dinner alone most evenings, and he had not been to a single one of his grandchildren's Saturday games.

He had built exactly what he said he did not want to build.

When a friend pushed him to sit down and actually compare his current picture with the one he wanted, the problem became visible. It was not that he was too busy. It was that he had never defined what "enough" looked like. He had no picture — which meant there was no signal telling him when the business had taken more than its share.

He cut his client list in half, raised his rates, and added Saturdays back to the family calendar. The business still works. So does the rest of his life.

The picture exercise is what prevents that drift before it starts.

---

The exercise today uses six categories: the people in your life and business, the activities that actually fill your time, the roles you carry, and then three that work differently — flavors, colors, and sounds.

Those last three are not decoration. They access how you feel about your situation in ways that direct description sometimes misses. If your life as it is today left a taste in your mouth — what would it be? What taste do you want it to leave? What would you hear if you listened to the current picture versus the one you want?

For each of the six categories, you fill two columns: as it is, and as I want it to be. The space between those two columns is the most important thing the exercise produces. It shows you where your energy needs to go, in ways that a list of business goals never will.

One instruction worth following exactly: complete the "as it is" column honestly. Not the version that sounds reasonable. The actual picture. This exercise only produces useful information if the current picture is accurate.

---

The business is meant to fit inside your life. Not the other way around. Everything you build this week — the schedule, the marketing, the money work — is built around a picture of what that life looks like.

Today you paint that picture.

Open Day 2. Complete both columns for each category. Do not skip the sensory prompts. When you are done, look at the gaps. The largest one is telling you something specific about what needs to happen.
