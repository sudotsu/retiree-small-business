# Essentials Day 3 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Day:** Day 3 — Putting Time in Checkmate
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~590 words

---

You have a picture of where you want to go. Now you have to deal with the one thing that determines whether you ever get there: time.

Not a conversation about work-life balance. A real, practical reconstruction of how your week is actually organized. What has your time, what needs it, and what can be eliminated. Then you rewrite the schedule.

---

There is a particular challenge that comes with retirement that does not get named often enough. When you were working, your schedule had a backbone — a job with hours that organized everything else around it. Retirement removes that backbone. For some people that is liberating. For others it creates drift: time expands to fill whatever space it is given, and suddenly there is no time for anything because there is no structure for anything.

A business adds a new demand to that equation. And without protected, predictable hours for the business, it will run on whatever is left after everything else — which is usually not much, and rarely at the right time.

Frank was seventy-one, retired three years, and started a small tax preparation service for other retirees in his neighborhood. He had the skills to do the work in his sleep. His first season was chaos: clients calling at all hours, files spread across the kitchen table, his wife navigating around his paperwork to cook dinner. He told himself it would settle down.

The second season was worse because he had more clients.

The problem was not the volume. It was that Frank had never established when the business existed and when it did not. No schedule meant the business had all of his time by default.

After the second season he built a template: business hours were 9 AM to 1 PM, Monday through Thursday, January through April. Calls returned during those hours. No files on the kitchen table after 1 PM.

The third season: his best revenue, his calmest household, four afternoons a week free. His wife said she had gotten her husband back. Frank said the schedule had not limited the business — it had made the business possible to keep.

---

What you build in today's exercise is a template, not a day-by-day planner. The architecture of a typical week, not the furniture arrangement. From this template, the detailed calendar flows.

Three questions worth thinking through before you fill in the grid:

What are your non-negotiable commitments? The things that must be on the schedule regardless of what the business needs — medical appointments, physical activity, family time, whatever keeps you functional and connected. If they are not on the schedule, the business will eventually crowd them out. That is not a prediction. It is a pattern.

What are your time-wasters? Every person has them. Some are obvious. Some are not. Name yours honestly.

What does the business actually need — in hours, specifically? Think through the core activities: customer contact, service delivery, administration, marketing. Estimate each one. Assign them to real blocks on the schedule.

The goal is not a perfectly optimized schedule. The goal is a schedule that is honest, sustainable, and actually reflects what matters.

---

Open Day 3. Fill in the grid honestly. Don't plan an aspirational week — plan the week you will actually keep.
