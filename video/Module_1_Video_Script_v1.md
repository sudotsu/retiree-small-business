# Module 1 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Module:** Knowing Yourself: Your Foundation and Decision-Making
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~7–7:30 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~920 words

---

Think back to a significant decision you made in the last ten years. Not a small one — something real. A move, a career change, a financial call, a family decision that required you to hold a lot of competing things at once and come to a conclusion.

You didn't flip a coin.

You considered who the decision would affect, and how. You thought about what it would cost — in ways that went beyond money. You measured it against your values. You thought about what certain people in your life would think, a few of whose opinions carry more weight than you'd necessarily choose. You may have sat with it longer than felt comfortable. Then you made the call.

That process — the internal system you've been using to make decisions that matter for most of your adult life — is what this week examines.

Not because there's something wrong with it. It's gotten you here. But if the business you build in this course is going to fit your life, it needs to fit the person making the decisions — and most people have used that system so consistently they've never stopped to describe how it actually works.

---

Here's the honest reason this module comes before everything else.

The most common reason small businesses stall — not fail, but stall — isn't a bad idea. It isn't bad timing or a difficult market. It's misfit. The person running the business has built something that works against their own grain. Against how they actually respond when things are slow, what keeps them going when the work is hard, what they genuinely believe about selling and money and worth. And they usually don't discover this until they're already in it, wondering why something they chose feels heavier than it should.

I've run my own businesses long enough to have watched this happen — in myself and in others. The people who struggle longest are almost always fighting themselves somewhere. Their idea is solid. Their effort is real. But the structure they've built asks them to operate against how they're actually wired, every day. It starts as a small friction. Over time it becomes significant.

This week builds the map so you can build with your grain, not against it.

---

If you've come through Module 0, your workspace is ready and your hour is on the calendar. That was orientation. This is the first week of real work.

Five lessons.

Lesson 1.1 opens with a drill. You'll list every influence that affects how you make decisions — people, values, habits, fears, beliefs — then narrow it to the three that consistently shape your most important choices. By the end, you'll have named something that most people carry without ever examining. That naming is the foundation of everything else this week.

Lesson 1.2 organizes what you found into four categories: your attitudes, beliefs, motivations, and the people in your life whose energy affects your thinking. For each one, you assess what helps, what sometimes creates friction, and what tends to get in the way.

Lesson 1.3 is the most demanding session of the week. Every default setting carries a positive dimension and a negative one. Optimism creates momentum — and can underestimate what a thing will actually take. Caution manages risk — and can prevent movement entirely. Lesson 1.3 looks honestly at both sides across all four categories. Plan 85 to 90 minutes for it. Try to do it in one sitting — what you bring to this exercise determines how useful the rest of the module is.

Lesson 1.4 makes it operational. You'll rewrite the settings that create the most friction — not to pretend to be a different person, but to give your better instincts more room. And you'll build a set of personal guardrails: short, plain rules you'll carry into every module that follows.

Lesson 1.5 is the decision gate. Before you move to Module 2, you'll run an honest readiness check across seven areas — time, energy, money, mindset, support, risk tolerance, and one more. The point isn't to earn permission. It's to make a clear-eyed decision about your real pace going into the next phase, and whether anything needs attention before you get there.

---

This week rewards honesty over speed.

The exercises work when you write what's actually true — not what sounds like the right answer, not what looks better on paper. You've been running this decision-making system for a long time. You know how it actually performs — in the decisions that worked and in the ones you'd make differently. The work here is to see it clearly enough to use it deliberately.

There's no right profile to land in. There's no wiring that makes someone better suited for building a business than another. There's only an accurate map of how you're built — and the more accurate it is, the less friction you carry into everything ahead.
