# Essentials Day 7 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Day:** Day 7 — Keeping Customers
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~590 words

---

Yesterday you built a strategy to find customers. Today you build the strategy to keep them.

These are different disciplines. Finding a customer requires visibility and a compelling offer. Keeping a customer requires something that is harder to systematize but entirely possible to build: a relationship. This is where most of the value in a small business actually lives.

---

Consider the arithmetic for a moment.

It costs five to eight times more to acquire a new customer than to keep an existing one. And a customer who buys from you twice a year at two hundred dollars a transaction, over ten years, is worth four thousand dollars. Twenty such customers: eighty thousand dollars in lifetime value — from people you already have.

Most small business owners never calculate that number. And because they never calculate it, they treat every customer interaction as a transaction rather than as an installment in a relationship that is worth far more than the single purchase in front of them.

Dorothy ran a small alterations shop for seven years with a steady stream of customers. She did good work. People came in, paid, and left. She never took their contact information. Never followed up.

When her landlord raised her rent in year eight and she raised her prices by fifteen percent, three of her twenty regular customers did not come back. She did not know why. She had no way to reach out.

When she finally calculated what each of those customers had been worth — four visits a year at eighty-five dollars over seven years — the number was twenty-three hundred and eighty dollars per customer. She had never looked at it that way before. Losing three of them felt different once she did the math.

Dorothy started keeping a simple log: name, contact, last visit, what they brought in. She began sending a brief personal note to customers she had not seen in sixty days. She started asking customers with specific alteration needs if they knew anyone else who needed the same type of work.

She recovered two of the three lost customers within a year. She gained eleven new regulars in the next two, almost all through referrals. The rent increase stopped being a crisis.

None of it required a marketing budget. It required paying attention to the relationships she had been leaving on the table for seven years.

---

Today's exercise covers five areas:

How you will turn leads into customers — the path from first contact to first purchase, and where people are likely to drop off.

The customer service that communicates you value each person — what you do when something goes wrong, how quickly you respond.

The lifetime value calculation — your actual numbers, not an estimate. What a retained customer is worth over time.

How you will encourage your best customers to refer others — specifically, the information you will give them, and whether you are actually asking.

How you will stay on customers' radar between purchases — two specific actions, with how often and what they will contain. Not "send a newsletter" — the actual plan.

---

The real value of your business is in the people you already have. Not in the next customer. In the ones who have already trusted you enough to buy from you once — and who will come back, and bring others, if you give them a reason to.

Open Day 7. Build the strategy.
