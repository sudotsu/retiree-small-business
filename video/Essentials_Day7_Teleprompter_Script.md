# Teleprompter Script — Essentials Day 7
# Keeping Customers

_(Camera on. Warm lighting. Chas seated, relaxed, direct eye contact.)_

---

Yesterday: finding customers.

Today: keeping them.

[pause 1s]

It costs five to eight times more

to acquire a new customer

than to keep an existing one.

[pause 1s]

A customer who buys from you twice a year at two hundred dollars,

over ten years,

is worth four thousand dollars.

[pause 1s]

Most small business owners never calculate that number.

And because they don't calculate it,

they treat every interaction as a transaction

rather than as an installment in a relationship

worth far more than the sale in front of them.

[pause 2s]

Dorothy ran an alterations shop for seven years.

Good work. Steady customers.

She never took their contact information.

Never followed up.

[pause 1s]

When she raised prices and three regulars didn't come back,

she had no way to reach out.

[pause 1s]

When she calculated what each regular had been worth —

twenty-three hundred and eighty dollars over seven years —

losing three of them felt different.

[pause 1s]

She started keeping a simple log.

Personal notes to customers she hadn't seen in sixty days.

Started asking for referrals.

[pause 1s]

Eleven new regulars in the next two years,

almost all through referrals.

[pause 1s]

None of it required a marketing budget.

[pause 2s]

Today's exercise covers five areas:

converting leads, customer service, lifetime value,

turning customers into advocates,

and staying on their radar between purchases.

[pause 1s]

The real value of your business

is in the people you already have.

[pause 1s]

Open Day 7.

[pause 2s]

_(fade out)_

---

### Production Notes

- **Run time:** ~2:00–2:15 at relaxed pace
- **Delivery:** the LTV arithmetic should be delivered slowly and clearly — let the numbers sink in; Dorothy's story is short and factual; the five-area list moves quickly; the close lands with quiet weight
