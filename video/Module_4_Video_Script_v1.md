# Module 4 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Module:** Marketing: Five P's in a Pod
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~7–7:30 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~920 words

---

There is a reason the marketing section of every small business course gets the most attention.

It is also the reason most people do it wrong.

Marketing has a reputation for being complicated — a discipline of funnels and analytics and demographic segments that requires a different kind of person than the one who runs the actual business. Decades of consultants and software companies have worked hard to sustain that reputation, because complexity is profitable for the people selling the solution. For you, it is just noise.

Here is what marketing actually is for a small business: five questions you need to answer about your product, your price, how it reaches customers, where it lives in their minds, and how you communicate about it. In order. That is the week.

---

Three weeks ago you mapped your internal wiring. Two weeks ago you built a picture of the life your business needs to fit inside and wrote five objectives to close the gap. Last week you built a schedule to give that work a real home.

All of that was infrastructure. This week the course turns outward — to the people you are building this for: your customers.

---

Before the lessons, one principle worth carrying into all five: marketing fails most often not because the message is wrong, but because the order is wrong. People skip to promotion — the ad, the post, the pitch — before they have done the harder thinking that promotion depends on. A promotional message built on an unexamined product, a price that was guessed at, a delivery path full of friction, and a position no one has thought about is technically a marketing activity. It is also why so many marketing activities produce nothing.

This week works in order. The promotional message you build in Lesson 4.5 will only be as strong as the product, price, propagation, and position you build before it. Promotion amplifies whatever is underneath it. Make sure what is underneath it holds up.

---

Five lessons.

Lesson 4.1 starts with a truth about your product that takes some honesty to sit with. Your product is your baby. You have spent time building it — your offer, your skills, your business concept. You care about it genuinely, and that care is real. But your customer does not weigh your product against how long you worked on it. They weigh it against everything else available to them that solves the same problem. And they are not polite in that comparison the way people are polite about babies.

The framework you will use is called RAW — Relevance, Accessibility, and Worth. Relevance: does your product actually address what your customer is trying to accomplish — not the service you provide, but the outcome they want? Accessibility: can they find you, understand what you offer in thirty seconds, and buy from you without friction? Worth: does what you charge feel like a fair exchange for what they receive?

You will evaluate your product on all three from your customer's perspective, then from your own, then name the gap between the two. That gap is the map of your marketing work — not a problem to be embarrassed about, but a specific set of things to address. The mini-story this week — Dorothy and her jam — illustrates what closing a gap looks like when you've identified the right one. Her product was not the problem. Her customers loved it. They just could not figure out how to get more once the market closed.

Lesson 4.2 is pricing. Most small business owners either underprice out of uncertainty or price without any grounding in what the customer actually values. The three-step process here establishes a working price grounded in what the customer gets, what it actually costs you, and what the market around you looks like. Plan 75 to 80 minutes — there is a valuation exercise in this lesson that requires real thinking, not just a number.

Lesson 4.3 maps the complete path your product takes from your business to your customer — every step, every handoff, every point where someone might meet friction and give up. Propagation is not just distribution. It is the entire experience of getting what you sell into the hands of the person who wants it. This lesson asks you to map your current path with full honesty, identify every friction point, and simplify where you can.

Lesson 4.4 is positioning: the place your business occupies in your customer's mind. Not the place you want to occupy — the place it actually occupies right now, across five dimensions, and the gap between those two things. You will use your Module 1 default settings in this lesson. The position that is genuinely sustainable for your business is one built on who you actually are, not a persona that fights your grain.

Lesson 4.5 is promotion — your message and how it travels. The lesson covers four components that correspond directly to the four P's you will have worked through: what you say about your product, how you signal your price, how you tell people to act, and how you describe your distinct place without mentioning a single competitor by name. It ends with a first draft advertisement — real language, real product, real ask. That exercise generates more community discussion than anything else this week. Share the draft, not the polished version. Unpolished feedback from someone who fits your customer profile is worth more than a polished ad that has never been tested.

---

By the end of this week you will have a complete marketing foundation: all five P's worked through specifically for your product and your customers, and a promotional message built from the ground up rather than borrowed from a template.

Download the Module 4 workbook before you open Lesson 4.1. Then start with your product through your customer's eyes. Be honest about the gap. That is where all useful marketing work begins.
