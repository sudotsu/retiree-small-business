# Essentials Day 5 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Day:** Day 5 — Cash and Profits
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~590 words

---

Today is the numbers day. Not projections. Not plans. Actual numbers — what is currently flowing into your business and out of it, and whether those two things add up to a business that can sustain itself.

Most people find at least one part of this exercise uncomfortable. Do it anyway. An honest financial picture — even an ugly one — is one of the most useful things you can have at the start of a business. A flattering picture that does not reflect reality is one of the most dangerous.

Before you open the lesson: pull out your bank statements and receipts from the last two or three months. You need them before you start, not once you are in the middle. Have them on the table.

---

There is a distinction this day will ask you to hold clearly that confusing is one of the most common ways small businesses fail.

Profit is what the books show. Cash flow is actual money — what is in your account right now, available to spend. A business can be profitable on paper and simultaneously unable to pay its bills, because revenue got recorded before it arrived and expenses came due before the money was there.

Gordon ran a woodworking shop and sold custom pieces at craft fairs and through repeat clients. By every measure he could see, the business was doing well. More orders than he could fill. Customers pleased. An award from a regional crafts association.

He had no budget and no cash flow tracking. He did not see the reason.

In October of his third year, he had a four-week stretch with no fairs scheduled and two clients who delayed their orders. Revenues dropped to near zero. But the fixed expenses kept running — the shop lease, the tools insurance, the supply account. He had been spending what came in and building no reserve because the books always looked fine.

He came very close to shutting down a business that was genuinely good.

After that, he built a simple budget and began tracking cash flow every month. He discovered his business had a predictable seasonal pattern he had never seen because he had never looked. October and February were always slow. He used that to build a reserve in his high months.

He has not had a cash crisis since. The business is the same. The information is different.

---

Day 5 has two parts.

Part one is the cash flow table: every dollar that actually deposited into your account over the last three months is cash in. Every dollar you actually paid out is cash out. No receivables you have not collected, no credit lines — real cash only. This gives you the physical reality of your financial position.

Part two is the budget: your revenues and expenses categorized and projected across three months. Fixed expenses are the ones that run every month regardless of activity. Variable expenses move with the business. Once you have both in view, the question the lesson asks you to answer directly is: what specific adjustments can you make in the next ninety days that would improve net profit?

Not "work harder." Specific adjustments. Revenue increases or expense reductions you can actually make.

---

This is the longest day of the course. Set aside uninterrupted time. Have your bank statements on the table.

Then open Day 5. Face the numbers. They are information, not a verdict.
