# Essentials Day 6 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Day:** Day 6 — You Need Leads to Succeed
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~590 words

---

You have a business and a financial picture. Now you have to answer the question that determines whether any of it goes anywhere: where are your customers going to come from?

More good businesses fail from insufficient lead generation than from bad products or poor service. The business works fine — there just are not enough people finding out about it. And that happens for a predictable reason: most people who start a business are energized by the thing they do well. Finding and attracting customers is different work — more uncomfortable, less familiar, easy to defer. So it gets deferred. And then deferred again.

Today you stop deferring it.

---

Rita started a resume and job-search coaching service after retiring from thirty years in human resources. She had the expertise. She had a strong professional network. She spent her first two months building a website, designing a logo, and getting her LinkedIn profile in shape.

At the end of month two she had one client — a friend who felt obligated.

She had been preparing to be found. She had not been generating leads.

A former colleague suggested a different approach. Instead of waiting, Rita identified twenty-five people in her network who worked in industries where mid-career transitions were common. She sent each of them a personal note — not a mass email, a personal note — explaining what she was doing and asking if they knew anyone who might benefit.

Within three weeks she had seven clients. Her website had not changed. Her rates had not changed. The only thing that changed was that she stopped waiting and started asking.

She still has a website. It has never been her primary source of clients. Her referral network has.

---

The exercise today lists twelve lead generation channels — word of mouth, newspapers, magazines, press releases, direct mail, telemarketing, trade shows, internet and social media, radio, television, billboards, and directories. For each one you answer two questions: Would I actually use this? Can I afford it right now?

Most channels will not make it through both filters. That is fine. You are not trying to do everything. You are trying to identify a short list of channels that are realistic for your business and your customer.

Word of mouth is first on that list for a reason. A referral from someone a potential customer already trusts arrives with built-in credibility that no advertisement can purchase. If you have existing customers and you are not actively asking for referrals, that is the most effective and least expensive change you can make today.

After you work through the twelve channels, the exercise asks for one specific action this week. Not a channel you intend to activate eventually. One action, named specifically, that you will take before the week is out.

The difference between "I will work on word of mouth" and "I will call three former colleagues by Friday and tell them I am taking clients" is the difference between an intention and a lead.

---

The work in today's lesson is not complicated. It is a decision, followed by a commitment to one action.

Open Day 6. Work through the twelve channels honestly. Then commit to one.

One well-chosen channel done consistently will outperform a dozen channels done sporadically. You need one channel that is genuinely right for your customer — and one action this week to activate it.
