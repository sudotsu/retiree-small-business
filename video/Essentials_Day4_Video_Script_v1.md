# Essentials Day 4 Video Script — Full Narrative (v1)

**Day:** Day 4 — Marketing and Promotion
**Instructor:** Chas (on camera)
**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 minutes at 125–130 WPM
**Word count:** ~590 words

---

Today the course turns outward.

Three days of looking inward — your wiring, your big picture, your time. Today you build the case for your business in the market: what it is, what it costs, how it reaches customers, and what makes it worth choosing over everything else available to them.

Marketing has a reputation for being complicated and expensive. That reputation mostly benefits the people who sell marketing services. For a small business, the fundamentals are not complicated. Getting them clear before you spend any money on promotion is the most effective thing you can do.

---

Vivian had run a catering operation out of her home for two years before she realized she had no idea why people were hiring her instead of the three other caterers in her area. She had customers. She could not tell you what was bringing them in.

A friend who had worked in advertising asked one question: who is your customer, exactly?

Vivian said, "Anyone who needs catering."

Her friend said: "That is not a customer. That is a population. Who, specifically?"

After an hour of conversation, what emerged was this: Vivian's actual customers were families planning retirement parties and milestone celebrations for people over sixty. She had been doing this for two years without knowing it. Her food was good — but what her customers were really buying was someone who understood that seventy years of life deserved a certain kind of celebration, and who treated the guests of honor with the dignity they had earned.

That was her positioning. It had been true the whole time. She had just never said it.

Once she could say it, her promotional materials changed, referrals increased, and she stopped competing for corporate lunches she was never going to win. She was exactly the right caterer for the ones that mattered most to her customers.

---

The framework for today is five words that start with P.

Product — what you are actually selling, in two sentences that a stranger would immediately understand. If you cannot explain it that clearly, that is the first thing to fix. Customers cannot buy what they cannot understand.

Price — what you charge and why that is the right amount. Price communicates value. Too low signals the product is not worth much. Too high without a clear reason will cost you customers. You do not need a final number today — you need a pricing rationale.

Propagation — how the product gets from you to the customer. Make the path to purchase completely clear. The most common mistake here is assuming the customer will figure it out.

Positioning — what makes your product the obvious answer for your specific customer. Not that you are better. That you serve a particular person or solve a particular version of the problem in a way nobody else does.

Promotion — how the public learns about you. This P comes last, and deliberately. Once the other four are clear, you can make smart decisions about where to spend. Promoting before the other four P's are worked out is one of the most expensive mistakes a small business makes.

---

Open Day 4. Work through each section specifically. Vague answers here produce vague results in the market. By the end of today you will have the marketing foundation your business needs before it spends a dollar on promotion.
